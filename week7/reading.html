Week 07 — Ideation & Prototyping

Reading Response

01

Reflection

I think the paper circuit design is really interesting, it allows many designs to be interactive with lighting on a flat surface. It allows us to make a quick prototype to see how the interaction changes. I also found the discussion of additive and subtractive manufacturing very useful because it encouraged me to think about how the making process shapes a design. I learned about this idea when I was doing sculpture back in high school, but before, I just see them as ways of making a 3D artwork. However, connecting with what we are doing in this class, building up material and removing it actually offer different ways to approach the same form. There can be several ways to construct one object, and the process of making could meant something. Just like we use trash to build endangered animal, that the way we built it leads to reflection.

Further, Snyder's “Paper Prototyping” had placed an emphasis on choosing an appropriate level of detail for prototyping. Personally, I have the habit of making only one final and make it perfect. I worked with 3D developing softwares when I was preparing my portfolio, I even model the physical space in ratio out to make sure that the installation works. But through our learning, I realized that I do not always need to begin with a precise CAD model or a finished print. I could first use paper or cardboard to explore size and movement, the physical prototype could give visual effect of the final and also allows me to experience with texture. I can then develop a more precise model when fit and construction become the main questions. I think it is important to treat these methods as complementary tools and choose between them according to what I need to communicate.

Victor raises a broader question about what kinds of interaction we want to design. His criticized futuristic screen interactions ask to the assumption that more advanced technology necessarily creates richer experiences. I agree to his point because this is sort of a spot light of what we do as designers and artists. I was often questioned why do you still like to make art by hand or will you be replaced by AI? I think his argument actually answers these questions for me in some way. When we are dealing with advanced technologies to display our artwork, we naturally assumes that the advanced ones creative a more immersive experience, and shows our innovative approach. However, I realized that being innovative is not about the platform or technologies, it is the idea. When we make prototypes by hand, we are being so creative because there are not limitations on methods and materials. Sometimes the limits on things we have might bring us a surprise on the result. Just as Victor mentioned, we are receiving much information through pressure, weight, texture, and movement. This means that I can evaluate an interface through its appearance while overlooking what the body experiences.
