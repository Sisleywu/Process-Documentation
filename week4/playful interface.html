Week 04 — Ideation & Prototyping

Playful Interface

Overcooked

01

Overcooked

Overcooked is a cooperative cooking game where players work together to prepare and serve dishes within a limited amount of time. Players collect ingredients, chop vegetables, cook food, assemble dishes, serve orders, and wash dishes.

02

Love Letter to Overcooked

Dear Overcooked,

I love you. I've played every versions and DLCs of you series, and no matter how chaotic and frustrating you can be, I always loved you. Your mechanics are simple and easy to understand, yet somehow you can turn the simplest cooking tasks into complete chaos.

I've played you with many of my friends, and some of my favorite memories were from these moments. We laugh when someone accidentally throws a food in the wrong direction; when two people prepare the same dish without realizing it; when everything goes wrong just before an order is due. Sometimes we are shouting instructions at each other, but we still enjoying playing and satisfied by completing a difficult level together. What I appreciate most about you is that you make cooperation feel both challenging and rewarding. You made communication chaotic but fun, and the process of reaching a goal is often much more memorable than the result itself.

Sometimes you can also be incredibly frustrating. Like I know exactly what I want to do, but my hands are not listening to my brain. I press the wrong button on my gamepad, pick up the wrong thing, or throw something in a completely different direction than I wished. It feels strange that such simple actions can become so difficult under pressure. You turn ordinary tasks like chopping foods and washing dishes into situations that require cooperation, quick reactions, and communication.

But strangely, I'm enjoying this chaos and I don't think I want you to become easier. I like the funny moments it creates by chaos, I like when me and my friends laugh about the mess we make. The failure in tasks and frustration makes success felt even more satisfying. When my friends and I finally complete a level after several failed attempts, the sense of accomplishment is much stronger. You always made me want to try again, not only because I want to achieve a higher score, but because I want to experience that moment of success together with my friends.

I also find it interesting how you change the way I interact with my friends. In daily life, cooking together might be a relatively calm and organized activity, but with you, it becomes a playful stage. During the gameplay, we constantly negotiate responsibilities, interrupt each other, and react to unexpected situations. As we played for a longer time, me and my friend cooperate perfectly without even speaking. Although there are times when we talk so much that nobody understands what is happening. You make communication fun and necessary, but you also make it difficult sometimes.

Maybe that is exactly what I love about you. You make simple actions into unexpected accidents , but you also turn mistakes into laughter, frustration into accomplishment, and make time into memories. I wish you understood that what I enjoy most is not just achieving the highest score, the experience of doing something together is the most important thing I found with you.

03

Identifying Opportunities

In this game, as levels goes up, the kitchen environments become increasingly complicated, introducing obstacles, moving platforms, and different spatial arrangements that require players to adjust their strategies. So even tasks are simple, we must constantly switch between different actions while paying attention to their surroundings, the remaining time, and what our teammates are doing. The goal is not just to complete individual tasks efficiently, but to coordinate with others to achieve a shared objective. Control, Communicate, and navigate are huge parts of this game that makes the mechanisms work.

The most outstanding quality of this game is its social ability. The experience is chaotic and unpredictable due to the combination of time pressure, moving environments, and multiple players requirement. This also makes communication and collaboration important, when we are playing this game, we are also socializing with each other.

When we are playing this game, we constantly exchange information and give quick reactions. The mechanisms make communication and recognition very important. I usually play Overcooked with a gamepad. Most interactions are controlled through a small number of buttons and directional inputs. The navigation, selection and movement are all controlled by digital systems and the gamepad.

Although Overcooked simulates cooking, most of the physical qualities of cooking are absent. Our interactions with ingredients and kitchen tools are done through the same buttons on gamepad, regardless of how they are different in reality. For example, ingredients have different visual appearances, but players cannot feel their textures, weights, temperatures, or resistance. The game requires cooperation, but each player operates a separate controller and our voices and interactions with one another do not directly affect the digital kitchen. We coordinate our decisions, but we rarely need to physically being engaged in the game.

Therefore, thinking of the qualities of Overcooked, I want to change the way of cognition. I wonder how Overcooked might change if the physical properties of cooking and cooperation became part of the interface itself.

04

Three Concepts

Concept 01

Environment - Sound Kitchen

Overcooked focused more on a visual experience, but while I play, I found out that each other's voices are important for us to communicate and cooperate. I wonder if I can make the sound more special to this experience, like what if sound and rhythm became part of the cooking process? For this concept, maybe players can create different sounds or synchronize their rhythms to chop, cook and serve food using different instruments or prototypes that creates different sounds. The overlapping sounds might create more confusion and unexpected results in the kitchen.

Concept 02

Social - “Collaborative” Kitchen

In Overcooked, we cooperate by controlling different chiefs doing different tasks to complete the dish together. The most efficient for me and my friends were making separate dishes or we divide tasks clearly. I wonder what if two players had to physically coordinate their movements to control one chef? In this situation, neither player could complete a cooking task independently, and every action required cooperation, shared movement, or synchronized gestures.

Concept 03

Material - Sensory Kitchen

In Overcooked, we recognize an ingredient by looking at its appearance. Sometimes I see the wrong item, or even if I recognize correctly, I press wrong button on my gamepad. I wonder if I can replace the visual elements with tactile recognition and manipulation. Like What if players interacted with ingredients through touch, texture, weight, and shape instead of buttons and visual icons? Then players had to identify ingredients by feeling them, and the physical properties of the materials determined how they were prepared in the kitchen.

05

Prototyping

Sensory Kitchen

I choose the Sensory Kitchen concept because it offers a unique way to interact with game elements through touch and texture, which could enhance the player's immersion and engagement.

Making Process

I found different types of materials for the sensory kitchen. Different types of fabrics, papers with different textures, bricks, fluffy balls, and strings. I choose the ones that have different feelings by touch, and cut them into small pieces.

I used some cardboard and bubble wrap to create the base structure for the sensory kitchen.

I created a drawer using cardboard, to hold the different materials, allowing players to open and close it to access the ingredients. I rolled a piece of fabric and tied it using string to represent an ingredient.

I glued the cardboard piece together to make the base structure for the sensory kitchen. They mimic the cooking tops in Overcooked. And then I stick different materials to create surfaces with different textures.

Final Prototype

This is my final prototype for the sensory kitchen. It has cook tops with different textures. Each texture is corresponding to a different type of ingredient in the game. Players will be able to interact with the different textures to simulate the sensation of handling various ingredients in the game. I tested out by making players blind, so that they can only rely on the tactile feedback from the different textures. This also can be done by connecting it with a sensor that detects touch and pressure, then the real Overcooked game will react upon player's choices.

Testing the prototype

06

Reflection

In the original Overcooked, players can only interact with ingredients through buttons, and different cooking actions uses similar inputs. With physical interfaces like this sensory kitchen, players can actually touch and hold the ingredients. Instead of simply recognizing ingredients by their appearance on the screen, players can identify their different textures, shapes, and weights through touching. This is what the digital interface cannot do, and it creates a realistic, immersive experience for players.

I think this introduces a more comprehensive interaction because players need to pay attention to the physical properties of foods inside the kitchen rather than just reacting to visual information. Which is more connected to cooking in a real world setting. It also makes the cooking process feel more tangible, while potentially making new challenges and unexpected mistakes because people may not recognize exact texture.

The materials is an important part of how I designed the interaction. I wanted different ingredients to bre recognizable by their physical qualities, so players could distinguish between them through touch. This made me think about how softness, roughness, shape, and size could communicate information without relying on visuals displayed on screen. Unlike pressing buttons on a controller, physically touching and picking up objects requires more hand movement and coordination. I also had to consider how easily players could recognize the ingredients by the feeling, especially when they were under time pressure. I realized that the materials are not just representations of ingredients, their physical properties can actually influence how players interact with the game.

One thing I wanted to observe during testing was whether players could recognize different ingredients as I expected. This actually works pretty well because the materials I choose feels very different, and I tested the feeling myself before making it. My friend who tested for me actually had a little bit of problem when she was trying it. I think this type of problem can be eliminated if this is a combination of touching and the screen, so that she can see. This made me interested in the difference between the designer's intention and the player's actual experience. Textures that seems obvious to me might be confusing to others. The realistic ingredients would make players more engaged with the cooking process or it can slow them down.

If I can continue develop this interface, I would experiment with a wider variety of materials and more cooking actions. Right now the main focus is recognizing the textures and feelings of different materials, but I think there is a great potential to explore actions such as squeezing, tearing, mixing, or assembling objects to represent different cooking processes. Because in real life I do so when cooking. I think it would be also interesting to explore how the physical interface could communicate more directly with the digital game. For example, placing an ingredient into a physical pot could be shown on the screen, or different physical gestures could affect cooking progress.
