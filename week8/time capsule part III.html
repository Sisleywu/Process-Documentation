WEEK 08 — IDEATION & PROTOTYPING

Time Capsule Part III:
The Instruction Manual

A speculative visual narrative about the discovery and misinterpretation of human artifacts in 2151.

For the final part of the Time Capsule project, I imagined what would I interpret these objects if I know nothing about them. I tried to think of how people living 125 years in the future might discover and interpret the objects preserved inside my cabinet. Without access to their original personal meanings, future researchers mistakenly classify these ordinary belongings as technological devices used by early 21st-century humans.

YEAR / 2151 FORMAT / SPECULATIVE INSTRUCTION MANUAL MEDIUM / FIGMA & DIGITAL DESIGN PLACE / Ancient NEW YORK, 10036

01 / CONCEPT DEVELOPMENT

Concept Development

Before creating the final visual narrative, I tried to think of them without prior knowledge, and adding more futuristic view. I think they might be more technological from 125 years later.

01 — ESTABLISH THE FUTURE

The Year 2151

The story takes place in 2151, 125 years after the creation of my time capsule. A group of future researchers discovers an unusual cabinet containing several objects from the early 21st century. Although the physical objects have survived, most information about their original purposes and personal significance has disappeared.

02 — CHOOSE A PERSPECTIVE

Future Researchers

The narrative is told from the perspective of researchers who specialize in reconstructing ancient human technologies. They believe the recovered objects belong to a complex technological system designed to regulate different aspects of human life.

03 — INTERPRET

Misunderstood Artifacts

Rather than recognizing the objects as personal belongings, the researchers assign each artifact a technological function based on its appearance, materials, and structural details. Their interpretations transform ordinary objects into fictional devices.

04 — CHOOSE A FORMAT

The Instruction Manual

I chose to present the researchers' interpretations through an instruction manual. By combining technical diagrams, component descriptions, and operating procedures, I wanted the fictional explanations to appear scientific and convincing, even though they are fundamentally incorrect. I think this will be funny because instructional manuals are somewhat serious, but it gives the completely wrong usage of objects.

Central Question: I wonder what happens to the meaning of an object when its physical form survives, but the personal memories and cultural context surrounding it disappear?

02 / NARRATIVE DEVELOPMENT

Storyboard

I developed a sequence that begins with the discovery of the cabinet and follows the researchers as they examine the recovered objects. Their observations gradually develop into a collection of speculative interpretations, which are eventually compiled into an official instruction manual.

NARRATIVE SEQUENCE

01 / DISCOVERY

A mysterious cabinet from the 21st century is recovered.

02 / EXAMINATION

Researchers open the compartments and examine the objects.

03 / ANALYSIS

Each object's physical features are documented.

04 / MISINTERPRETATION

Researchers assign fictional technological functions.

05 / RECONSTRUCTION

The findings are compiled into an official operating manual.

03 / FINAL VISUAL NARRATIVE

Human Operating Manual

The final outcome is a fictional instruction manual published in 2151. Each recovered object is presented as an ancient human technological device, complete with a reconstructed function, component analysis, operating instructions, and safety warnings. The manual uses a futuristic visual language and simple to create an illusion of scientific authority.

Through this manual, familiar personal belongings become mysterious technological artifacts. The visual format suggests precision and objectivity, while the reconstructed functions reveal how easily incomplete information can produce convincing but inaccurate narratives.

04 / CRITICAL REFLECTION

Reflection

For the final part of my Time Capsule project, I imagined my cabinet being discovered by researchers in the year 2151. Since the personal stories and cultural context behind the objects have been lost, the researchers attempt to reconstruct their functions based entirely on their physical appearances. I chose to present their interpretations through a fictional instruction manual, where ordinary personal belongings are classified as technological devices with specific functions and operating procedures.

I chose the format of an instruction manual because I thinked of when I was being introduced to something brand new. I think instruction manual is a way of how we learn about new things, we learn about how to constuct, what function it takes and what are the boundaries. I think the contrast between scientific authority and misunderstanding is interesting. Instruction manuals are usually designed to communicate information clearly and precisely. By presenting incorrect interpretations through technical diagrams, component descriptions, and operating instructions, I wanted to explore how convincing an explanation can appear even when it is based on incomplete evidence.

Comparing Part I and Part III, I noticed that the physical characteristics of the objects were preserved, while their personal meanings were often distorted or transformed. For example, my Little Sheep originally represented companionship and the memories of traveling with it. In the future researchers' interpretation, it becomes a device that protects humans from supernatural creatures during sleep. Although its function is completely misunderstood, the idea of protection still reflects the sense of comfort and security I associate with it.

Similarly, the handmade cup originally represented a personal relationship and a memory of someone important to me. In Part III, it becomes a relationship transmitter that allows humans to exchange emotional energy. Although the researchers misunderstand how the object works, they unintentionally preserve its connection to human relationships. I think this is highly realated to creative processes, that we all make different interpretations and assumptions. There is no universal right and wrong, but the form of communication and context is important. Like the format of instruction manual would automatically make us feel authoritative. Therefore, I think the medium and way of expression is important, that it generates meaning and makes the artwork different. We should reflect about how we create, think about platforms of presentation and combine them together to make a comprehensive expression.

I also found it interesting that some interpretations reveal more about the imagined future society than about the objects themselves. For example, the clothing tags are interpreted as a human classification system, where barcodes, prices, and sizes determine a person's identity and social value. This interpretation exaggerates the relationship between consumerism and identity in contemporary society. What appears to be a misunderstanding also becomes a form of social commentary.

Through this project, I realized that preserving an object does not necessarily mean preserving its original meaning. Physical materials, shapes, and patterns may survive for centuries, but the personal experiences and relationships connected to them can disappear. At the same time, meaning is not simply lost; it can also be reconstructed and transformed through the perspectives of those who encounter the objects. Every aspect of an artwork would mean something. When we are in a creative process, we need to think about every step and experiment. Because all of which matters, it forms information and knowledge during this ideating process.
